## Edit your profile

Open **Settings** using your profile shortcut. Set your preferred name and time zone, then add any goals, training details, or food preferences that help you keep a useful workspace. Save your changes and wait for confirmation.

Profile editing is optional after account creation. Your preferred name is not an account migration tool; changing it does not move another person's records into your workspace.

## Set optional nutrition targets

In **Settings → Nutrition targets**, select **I want to set my daily targets**. Enter your chosen calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat for **Training days** and **Recovery days**, then save.

You can track food without setting targets. Unset values mean no chosen target, not a recommendation of zero. Corner does not calculate a dietary prescription from your goals.

**Your usual week** helps select daily targets but does not schedule or log workouts automatically. Add specific workouts in the planner when you want a dated session. Keep your actual training and the week's default pattern distinct.

## Review training progress

Open **Training → Progress**. Review completed sessions, actual minutes, completed working sets, session effort, and lift history. Use the selected week and the displayed date range to understand which records a view includes.

Missing records and missing values are not the same as zero performance. A blank effort rating is not an effort of zero. A planned workout is not a completed session.

## Add a body or recovery check-in

In **Training → Progress → Recovery & measurements**, choose **Check in**. Check the date, then add at least one measurement, rating, or note and save.

- Body weight uses **lb** and waist uses **inches**, independently of your preferred workout load unit.
- Sleep is recorded in hours.
- Energy, soreness, and boxing-quality ratings use the form's 1–5 scale.
- Leave an unrecorded value blank rather than inventing a zero.

Use check-ins and notes as context when looking back. The views summarize your saved records; they do not diagnose recovery or replace professional advice.
